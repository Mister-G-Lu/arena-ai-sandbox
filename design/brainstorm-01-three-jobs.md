# Brainstorm 01 — Three Job Concepts for a Simulation-Crack Incremental

**Genre:** Fallen London-style action economy (≈50 actions/day, every action = a storylet
with a snippet of text, sometimes repetitive) × incremental game (qualities, unlocks,
prestige loop).
**The lie:** You coordinate a simple, mundane job.
**The truth:** The world is a simulation, and your job makes you the one person who
sees the seams.

---

## Shared skeleton (applies to all three)

Because the same machinery powers every variant — this is the frame the job plugs into.

- **The Loop.** ~50 actions/day. Each action = a storylet: a card with 2–4 lines of text
  and a result. Repeatable "grind" cards advance job-related qualities; unique
  "story" cards fire when qualities cross thresholds. Repetition is *diegetically fine*
  here — routine is the camouflage the mystery hides inside.
- **Qualities = your suspicion, quantified.** FL tracks stats like *Watchful*; we track
  *Noticed Things*. Every glitch you catch raises it. It never goes down (except it
  sometimes does, and that's a glitch too — see below).
- **Glitches escalate in tiers.** Tier 0 = "huh, weird" (a typo, a déjà vu). Tier 1 =
  "that contradicts the last thing" (a door that wasn't there). Tier 2 = "physics is
  apologizing" (the sky loads late). Tier 3 = "it knows you saw it." Tier 4 = "you can
  touch the source." You must *invest* actions to escalate a glitch — grinding a glitch
  is the core loop, and deeper investigation costs more.
- **The prestige loop is the plot.** Resetting the incremental IS the simulation
  rebooting. Permanent currency across reboots = **Residual Memory** — the world forgets,
  you don't. Every reboot, the world is a little more wrong *and* you start a little
  more awake. The meta-game is: how many reboots until the simulation notices you're
  carrying memory across the reset? (That's the endgame boss fight.)
- **The action economy is a simulation limit.** Why ~50 actions? Because *your attention
  is a resource the simulation throttles*. Late-game reveals: buy more actions per day —
  and find out what (or who) grants them.

---

## Idea A — **Dispatcher** *(you route the city's logistics)*

**The job:** You work the graveyard shift at the Central Dispatch Office of a vast,
slightly-off arcology city. You assign garbage trucks, maintenance crews, streetlights,
plumbing repair. Ledgers, manifests, work orders, radio calls. Pure bureaucracy —
Papers, Please energy.

**Why this job cracks the sim:** Your entire job is *reading the world's internal
records*. A simulation's records are its source of truth — so the first place the
source of truth goes wrong is in front of your face, filed under "clerical error."

**The glitch grammar (escalating):**
- *Tier 0:* The population count is always 31,412. The same work order comes in daily —
  "basement light, Building 7" — always filed, never fixed, no Building 7 on the map.
- *Tier 1:* You route a truck to Sector 9 and the driver radios back: "There is no
  Sector 9. We're in Sector 9." The manifest lists cargo you didn't authorize. Deliveries
  arrive *before* you dispatch them.
- *Tier 2:* You find a file on *yourself* — with tomorrow's date on it. A whole shift's
  paperwork is blank, but everyone remembers it vividly.
- *Tier 3:* The intercom reads your dispatches back to you before you speak them. A work
  order arrives addressed from the Dispatch Office, in your handwriting, asking you to
  stop looking at the paperwork.
- *Tier 4:* The records room door is open at night. Inside, the ledgers don't record the
  city — they *are* the city. Cross out a street and watch it erase from the window.

**Incremental layer:** Your dispatch desk upgrades (more trucks, faster routing =
automation of the grind). New districts unlock like new zones. Quality *Ledger-Keeping*
vs *Rule-Breaking* — you can file glitches as errors (safety, slow) or follow them
(risk, revelation). Endgame: you start *dispatching the simulation itself*.

**Vibe hook:** "The universe is a spreadsheet, and you're the one with read/write
access." Perfect thematic fusion of incremental game (numbers, spreadsheets!) with
narrative (the spreadsheet is the horror).

---

## Idea B — **Night Concierge** *(you maintain one impossible building)*

**The job:** You're the sole night-shift concierge of Meridian Tower — a decaying
brutalist residential megatower, 200+ floors. You handle packages, maintenance tickets,
resident requests, and the lobby log. Every night is a shift; every shift is a set of
rooms, hallways, and faces.

**Why this job cracks the sim:** You are the *continuity system* of the building. You're
paid to know what should be where, who should be when. When the world fails to persist —
that's your job description noticing it.

**The glitch grammar (escalating):**
- *Tier 0:* You're certain there were 204 floors yesterday. The elevator panel says 205.
  A resident's name is slightly different than last week. Nobody else notices; nobody
  else works nights.
- *Tier 1:* Room 1307 has a different tenant every night, and every tenant insists
  they've lived there for years. A hallway loops when you walk it — you pass Room 1102
  three times. The stairwell lights turn on *ahead* of you, floor by floor.
- *Tier 2:* You deliver a package to Floor 13 — the building has no 13th floor, but
  tonight it does. The resident signs, thanks you, and the floor is gone by the time
  you look back. The lobby log has an entry for you checking in — you never check in,
  you live here.
- *Tier 3:* The building re-arranges itself between your rounds, and only your logbook
  remembers. You start leaving marks — chalk, thread, tape — and they persist longer
  than walls do.
- *Tier 4:* The roof. The building is taller at night. From the roof, the city below is
  dark except for the blocks it's currently rendering. The tower isn't in the city —
  the city is *inside the tower's render distance*.

**Incremental layer:** Classic floor-crawler incremental — floors as zones, room cards
as repeatable grind, keys/elevator codes as gating. *Familiarity* quality per floor:
grind a floor until you know it by heart, *then* the glitches on it become visible —
mechanically, "looking closer" = investing actions. Prestige: the tower "renovates"
(reboots) and the floor plan is different — but your logbook keeps the old one. Two maps
in your head = the whole game.

**Vibe hook:** cozy-horror, *The Shining* meets *House of Leaves* meets an incremental.
One building, infinitely wrong. Great for slow drip: every night, 50 actions, one shift,
one new impossible thing.

## Idea C — **Courier** *(you walk the city that keeps moving)*

**The job:** You're a delivery courier in a megacity so big nobody has seen all of it.
Routes, packages, drop-offs. You know the streets better than anyone — which is a
problem, because the streets know you're watching.

**Why this job cracks the sim:** A simulation only renders what someone observes.
You're the city's most mobile observer. You see *where the rendering stops*.

**The glitch grammar (escalating):**
- *Tier 0:* Shortcut you've used for years is a dead end today — and always was. The
  delivery depot moved one block overnight, nobody moved it, everyone agrees it's
  always been there.
- *Tier 1:* Two identical blocks, one street apart — same barber, same cat, same
  graffiti, same *person* crossing at the same time. Walk a route you don't know and
  the city fills it in behind you a half-second late. Your map app's ETA counts down,
  then loops.
- *Tier 2:* A whole district you've never heard of appears between your stops — people
  live there, they know you, they're expecting this package. On the return trip:
  vacant lots. The package is addressed to *you*.
- *Tier 3:* Get lost *on purpose* and the city can't keep up — facades, repeating
  assets, NPCs who stop mid-sentence and wait. You learn to navigate by the glitches.
  The more deliberately you walk the wrong way, the more the city's grid is a lie.
- *Tier 4:* The city has an edge. There's a last street. Past it, the ground just...
  hasn't been written yet. But someone is writing, and the packages you carry are
  being delivered to the writer.

**Incremental layer:** A route-map as the main UI — the map IS the game board (killer
visual identity: the map starts lying as you progress). Zones unlock like districts;
automation = hiring couriers / drones. *Miles Walked* as XP, *Detours Taken* as the
rebel stat. Prestige: the city "re-zones" (reboots) — grid changes, but your mental
map persists; you can walk old routes that no longer exist.

**Vibe hook:** *Death Stranding* cargo-run zen × the most paranoid walking-sim ever.
The strongest "the map is the interface" gimmick of the three — very screenable,
very incremental-native.

## Quick comparison

| | Job | Seam it exposes | Daily loop | Endgame fantasy | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Dispatcher | The world's *records* | Ledgers, work orders, radio | You edit the source | Most text-heavy |
| **B** | Night Concierge | The world's *persistence* | Floors, rooms, residents | The tower *is* the renderer | Smallest world — needs depth |
| **C** | Courier | The world's *geometry* | Routes, maps, deliveries | You walk off the map | Biggest build (city content) |

## Open design questions (for next round)

1. **How visible is the mystery?** Fully hidden (player discovers the twist themselves
   over days) vs. advertised (blurb says "the world is a simulation," tension is *how*
   it plays out)? Hidden is more magical; advertised retains players longer.
2. **How dark?** Fallen London is arch and funny. Do glitches *menace* you (hostile
   simulation) or *ignore* you until it's too late (indifferent simulation)? Indifference
   is scarier and cheaper to write.
3. **What do the 50 actions *feel* like?** FL-style cards with choices, or simpler
   one-tap chips you batch through on the bus? (Affects how much text each snippet can
   carry.)
4. **The reset is the plot** — do we embrace that fully? i.e., the first reboot is a
   scripted, unavoidable story beat, not a player choice?
